The Problem

Crackdown set out to solve procrastination — but an anti-procrastination app risks becoming just another guilt-driven productivity tool if it's not careful. Before any interface decisions got made, the team needed to understand who actually struggles with habit-building, and why most existing tools fail them.

Research & Process

I led go-to-market strategy for the app, running 100+ user interviews alongside a full competitive analysis of existing habit and productivity tools. That research surfaced two things: a clear target segment — students and early-career people juggling goals across school, fitness, and job searching — and a specific, measurable failure point. Users were creating accounts and then abandoning the app before completing a single meaningful action: a steep signup-to-first-use drop-off.

Design Decisions

I owned the redesign of that onboarding-to-first-use flow, taking it through 5+ Figma prototype iterations. The core tension throughout was behavioral: how do you keep someone accountable to their goals without the app feeling punishing? That question shaped decisions across the product — from the tone of the sign-up flow, to a Goal Board that visualizes progress across multiple goals at once with a "Productivity Brief" chart plus per-goal streaks and time-to-deadline, to a calendar and daily task view that ties every to-do back to the goal it serves.

Small habit-reinforcing details were deliberate: streak counters that celebrate consistency instead of flagging misses, an "Inspiration Board" on each goal page for motivation, and progress framed around what's been accomplished rather than what's overdue. Every one of those was an attempt to make accountability feel motivating instead of judgmental.

Outcome

The go-to-market strategy and prototype won $500 at the Cozad New Venture Challenge, validated by both user interview findings and competition feedback.

What I'd Do Next

Given more runway, the next step would be instrumenting the redesigned onboarding flow with real usage analytics — to confirm the signup-to-first-use drop-off we identified in research actually closed, rather than relying on interviews and prototype feedback alone.
