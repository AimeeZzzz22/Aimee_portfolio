Download Full Pitch Deck (PDF)

The Problem

Most people aren't social media literate. 82% of middle schoolers can't distinguish a news story from sponsored content, and falsehoods spread 70% faster than the truth. In a 54-hour Startup Weekend sprint, our team set out to prototype something that could actually change that — not just tell kids to "be careful online."

Process

As Data Analyzer & Visual Designer on the team, I researched the competitive landscape — existing school media-literacy curricula and internet-safety programs — to find where they fell short: too broad, not classroom-ready, or narrowly focused on internet safety rather than misinformation and manipulation tactics. That research shaped the direction. Instead of another lecture-based program, we built toward something interactive that students would actually want to use.

Design Decisions

I designed the visual identity and the prototypes for the product's core teaching moments. The centerpiece is a choose-your-own-adventure style game: instead of being told that ads and influencer content manipulate audiences, students play as an advertising director or influencer themselves — choosing between a photoshopped image and an unedited one, fear-based messaging and factual messaging — and immediately see the engagement numbers each choice earns. The gap between the manipulative option and the honest one is the lesson, not a caption explaining it.

I also designed the explainer video concept and the interactive quiz format that reinforces each module, asking students to identify sources and fact-check claims in real posts rather than answer abstract questions.

Outcome

Built in 54 hours from concept to a pitched product, with a costed business model (B2B to schools, priced per-student or per-session) and a defined market: $75M potential revenue across 23,529 secondary schools nationwide.

What I'd Bring to a Team

Working under a 54-hour deadline taught me to ground product decisions in research fast rather than get stuck designing in a vacuum — the competitive analysis I ran directly shaped what we built, instead of happening after the fact to justify it. That's the instinct I'd bring to a team: validate the direction with real users or real market data before investing further, even under tight timelines.
